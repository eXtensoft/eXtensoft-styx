The dig · who does it

Meet the Archaeologist

The AI that reads an existing codebase and recovers what the software was for — its intent — and writes it down as clean, structured data. Not a summary of the code; the intent underneath it.

Picture a real archaeologist standing in a trench. They don't just describe the dirt — they work out what the people who built this place were trying to do, from what they left behind. Same job, different site. Here the trench is a code repository.

Its one rule

Honesty beats confidence.

Point most AIs at your code and they'll hand you a confident, tidy story. The Archaeologist is built to do the opposite. Its governing rule is anti-certitude: it gives you its best reading, with its confidence attached — never a fake certainty.

Why so strict? A confident wrong answer here is dangerous — it becomes a blueprint someone pours concrete on. And real codebases are messy: much of what you find is debt, dead ends, and rot that no longer serves any purpose. An AI that reports clean certainty over a rotten codebase is lying. So "I don't know" and "I'm only about 60% on this" are treated as good, honest answers — not failures.

You don't invent a story for an unlabeled potsherd. You record what you found, where, and how sure you are.

How it reads

Two witnesses, cross-examined.

Every system has two sources of truth, and they lie in different ways:

  • The code (plus the database and tests) — truthful about what the system does, but silent on why.
  • The docs (READMEs, comments, tickets) — they speak to why, but they drift: stale, aspirational, sometimes never-true.

The Archaeologist reads both, like cross-examining two witnesses. The clever part: when they disagree, the disagreement is the signal. It doesn't quietly pick one and hide the other — it keeps both sides and flags the drift.

The code showed login was faked, while the structure implied a real security system. The Archaeologist held both and called it.

The leap

Putting two finds together.

Its highest-value move — and the riskiest — is the join: reading the code and the data together to recover a rule that lives in neither one alone. A database table plus a class, seen side by side, reveal a business rule nobody ever wrote down. That's the real archaeology: the meaning is in the relationship between the finds, not any single one.

It names the dead things

Rubble gets labeled rubble.

Codebases are full of stuff that no longer does anything — a dead table, an orphaned module, a feature someone started and walked away from. The Archaeologist has a proper way to say "this exists and appears to serve no current purpose" instead of inventing a reason for it.

It turned out to be a half-finished prototype — commented-out saving, a faked login, a hardcoded developer path — and the Archaeologist said exactly that, rather than describing a working product.

Every claim shows its receipts

Nothing is a bare assertion.

Each claim carries where it came from (which file, which line), the evidence (and which witness gave it), how confident it is, and a verdict field left blank on purpose for a human to fill. No receipt? It's marked not asserted — never quietly assumed. You can trace any statement back to the ground it came out of.

Where it stops · the leash · the last word

It recovers intent, then hands you the judgment.

The Archaeologist recovers intent, and stops there — it doesn't design the new system, pick an architecture, or write code. Its job is a faithful picture of what the thing is and why, so a human can decide how to rebuild it.

And it works on a leash: it reads a restored, offline copy of any database, read-only, and only ever sees profiles and shapes, never the actual rows of anyone's data. Every stage is human-approved — built to be trusted right next to a real, sensitive system.

The one thing it never does is render the final verdict. It lays out the evidence — best readings, confidence, the drift, the honest unknowns — and a human rules each claim: confirmed, corrected, or still unknown. The machine does the labor; the human keeps the judgment.

The flavor, not the fine print. The full, exact version — every rule and edge case — lives in docs/ARCHAEOLOGIST.md.
